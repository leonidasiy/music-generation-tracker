# Instrument and chord improvement loop — 31 October 2026

## Executive summary
Temporal chord smoothing reduces fragmentation but does not reduce the primary guitar recording-failure count. Four newly admitted classical whole songs were inferred and scored with predictions sealed before reference access; richer chord-content matching does not uniformly improve triad classification. The instrument crop experiment now has 897 model forwards and independently reconstructed six-arm outcomes; publication of these research results is not production adoption or broad sub-30% confirmation.

## Contradictions and gaps
- Smoother chord displays do not automatically mean fewer failed recordings. Root and quality remain separate; forgiving inversions does not waive quality errors.
- The four external recordings share one composer, performer and song cycle. Exact checkpoint overlap is unknown; one song contains a publisher-created repeat. This is a bounded external diagnostic, not broad unseen-population confirmation.
- Explicit extended-pitch references were chosen prospectively. They disagree with shorthand labels for 1.14 seconds; neither representation was selected for favorable scores.
- Instrument references are partial clip labels, not exhaustive whole-song or section-audibility inventories. All fixed arms and regressions are retained rather than reporting only an attractive result.
